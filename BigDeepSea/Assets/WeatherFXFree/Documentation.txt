Weather FX Free

Documentation. 3 animated 2D weather effects, each one shader drawn by one WeatherFX
component: no textures, no particles.

  Resources/Weather FX Free/   the shaders, one per effect
  Scripts/WeatherFX.cs   Add, Set, Place, FadeIn, Stop
  Scripts/WeatherSystem.cs   weather states, cross-faded: Change, Weather, the forecast
  Demo/WeatherFXFreeDemo.unity   a pixel-art landscape in 12 moods and a toggle per effect; its code
                         in Scripts/Demo/, its landscapes in DemoArt/

Use:
  var rain = WeatherFX.Add("rain");          // covers the camera's view and follows it
  WeatherFX.Add("fog").Set("density", 0.8f).FadeIn(3f);
  rain.Stop(2f);                             // fades out, then destroys its object

Or add a WeatherFX component to an object and pick its Effect: it shows in the editor too. To keep
settings in the scene, make a material of the effect's shader and give it to the component.

Effects (WeatherFX.Effects lists them): rain, snow, fog.

With Size at zero an effect covers the main camera's view (orthographic or perspective, at the
object's depth) and follows it, its pattern staying put in the world.
A Size in units draws a rect from the transform, its top-left corner, right and down.

Settings: Set("density", 0.8f), Set("color", Color.white), Get("wind"): the shader's properties, by
their snake_case names ("ground_fog") or the shader's ("GroundFog", "_GroundFog"). Every effect has
Intensity (0 to 2, which FadeIn and Stop drive), Speed, Seed (Add picks one at random), ColorSteps
and PixelSize. Patterns are sized in world pixels, 100 to a unit (WeatherFX.PixelsPerUnit).

Drawing order is the renderer's sorting order: Add gives each effect 100 and up in the order above,
later ones over earlier ones.

Rain splashes where it meets its "ground" (0 to 1 down its rect; 1 for none).

Pixel art: set WeatherFX.DefaultPixelSize = 4 once and every effect draws in 4x4 world-pixel
blocks with hard-edged alpha (or give one effect its own PixelSize). ColorSteps posterizes.

Weather states: put a WeatherSystem on an object and, in play, Change("storm", 6f) fades out the
effects the state doesn't have and fades in the ones it does (WeatherSystem.States; Offered lists
the ones this package has). Weather sets it over Fade; Ground places the rain's splashes and the
lightning; a forecast of weighted states changes it on its own every ChangeEvery seconds.

Needs Unity 6.3 or later, in any render pipeline: the shaders are unlit, tested in Built-in, URP
(2D and Universal Renderer) and HDRP. The demo runs on either input handler.
